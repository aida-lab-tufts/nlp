- **Think Small: When your LLM needs to go on diet or Less is More: Intelligence of Small Language Models**, Sindhuja Thogarrati, Machine Learning Software Engineer, Bose Corp. 
  - **Abstract**: The current hype is all about LLMs and how many generic tasks it can perform. The quality of an LLM is determined by how well it can generalize over multiple tasks. It is also assessed in terms of how quick it can come up with an answer and the how satisfying it seems to the user. As a result it uses a lot of GPU/CPU compute and in some cases costs extra to access a better model. SLMs demand is to offset these costs and get to a better space where the inferencing can be done utilizing lesser compute for better results.
  - **Speaker Bio**: Sindhuja Thogarrati is a Machine Learning Software Engineer at Bose Corporation. She works with the ML research and product teams to optimize and profile audio ML algorithms on new hardware. Currently, she is working on ML research in the acoustics modeling domain. Her technical interest is in audio, speech and text processing on edge devices. In her previous career, she developed enterprise level solutions using NLP. Sindhuja holds a Master of Science (MS) in Computer Science from the University of Houston, and is currently an industry mentor at Tufts AIDA lab. 

- **Building AI Scientists for Therapeutic Discovery**, Zhenglun Kong, Postdoctoral Researcher,Harvard Medical School
  - **Abstract**: Recent advances in large language models are transforming how we approach biomedical discovery and scientific reasoning. In this talk, Zhenglun will discuss his efforts to build AI scientists—intelligent agents capable of multi-step reasoning, real-time knowledge retrieval, and autonomous interaction with diverse computational tools to support data-driven exploration and discovery. These agents operate within unified ecosystems that connect hundreds of models, datasets, and APIs, enabling dynamic tool selection, structured function calling, and cross-domain reasoning. By leveraging these systems, his team develops agents that can analyze molecular, pharmacological, and biological evidence to derive actionable insights and generate reliable hypotheses. Beyond domain-specific applications, these agents represent a broader paradigm for democratizing AI-driven science, where reasoning models autonomously discover, validate, and refine knowledge using transparent and interoperable frameworks. Zhenglun will also highlight methods for improving the efficiency, reliability, and interpretability of large models through adaptive fine-tuning and inference optimization. Together, these advances bring us closer to general-purpose AI scientists that can collaborate with humans in accelerating scientific innovation.
  - **Speaker Bio**: Zhenglun Kong is currently a postdoctoral researcher in Biomedical Informatics at Harvard Medical School, working closely with Marinka Zitnik. He received his Ph.D. from Northeastern University. He was recognized as a Machine Learning and Systems Rising Star in 2024. His research focuses on developing AI agents for clinical and health applications, as well as AI models for single-cell analysis. He is also dedicated to improving the efficiency and reliability of AI models through methods such as accelerating pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference, as well as model compression.
  
- **Towards Efficient, Trustworthy and Reasoning Generative Models**, Yize Li, PhD Student ECE, Northeastern University
  - **Abstract:** Generative models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), have demonstrated remarkable capabilities but simultaneously face significant challenges in efficiency, trustworthiness, and advanced reasoning. This talk explores three research frontiers aimed at addressing these issues. First, we introduce Machine Unlearning for building trustworthy AI and discuss how unlearning techniques can efficiently eliminate undesirable data influences from pretrained models without the prohibitive cost of full retraining, positioning it as a pivotal tool for the life-cycle management of LLMs. Second, we investigate Diffusion Language Models (dLLMs), a paradigm shift from traditional autoregressive generation. dLLMs enable parallel decoding, achieving significant inference acceleration and their inherent bidirectional attention mechanism offers superior, fine-grained control over output, enhancing both model utility. Finally, we discuss deeper abilities through Adaptive Reasoning, where LLMs learn to dynamically adjust their reasoning strategies based on task complexity, moving beyond static pathways to achieve more efficient problem-solving. All these techniques paves the way for the next generation of generative AI.
  - **Speaker Bio:** Yize Li is currently 5th-year ECE Ph.D. candidate at Northeastern University in Boston, where he is under the guidance of Prof. Xue Lin. His research interest lies at shaping the next generation of AI algorithms for efficiency, scalability and trustworthy in areas such as large language models, diffusion models and multimodal AI.
